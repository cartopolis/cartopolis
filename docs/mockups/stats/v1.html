cartopolis stats: how the world is used, and who visits the site

A fourth admin area, Stats, beside People, Content and Data. It counts two things the server can already see — people in the world, and visits to cartopolis.org — and keeps counts, not people: no cookies, no IP addresses, no third-party script, no exact positions. Everything on this sheet is new; the figures are invented.

1 · Where it comes from

Three sources

  • The simulator: each sign-in, refused handshake and session end (app, version, length), what it relays (a count per kind, never the content), and which 250 m square each avatar is in, once a minute.
  • The web pages: one small request per page shown — cartopolis.org, about.cartopolis.org, the viewer — with the page, where the visitor came from and the screen size.
  • Caddy, only for the APK: a download has no page to send a request from.
2 · Counting

In the simulator, in memory

  • A visitor is a hash of the day's secret, the IP address, the browser and the site. The secret is made at midnight, never written down, and thrown away the next midnight — so today's visit cannot be linked to yesterday's, and the IP goes no further than the hash.
  • Bots fall out on their own: they do not run the page's script. Requests from known crawlers are counted as "bots filtered" and nothing else.
3 · Keeping

stats.db, beside world.db

  • Its own file, not tables in the world: stats grow every minute, need no backup with the world, and can be wiped without touching anything else.
  • Sessions (account, app, start, end) are kept 90 days, then folded into daily totals — that is what "came back" and "active this week" need.
  • Web: only daily totals per page, source, country and device. No single visit is ever stored. Totals are kept for good.
4 · Showing

Admin › Stats

  • Four pages: Overview, World, Web and Places, on /api/admin/stats/*, behind a new stats permission, so a role can see the numbers without managing accounts.
  • Every table downloads as CSV.

What it never keeps

  • IP addresses — used for the hash and the country at the moment of the request, then dropped.
  • Cookies or ids in the browser — so the site needs no consent banner.
  • Message content — it is encrypted; the server counts what it relays and cannot read it.
  • Where a person was — positions are rounded to a 250 m square on the server and only the square's total is kept; a square appears only once 5 different people have been in it.
  • Which person read which page — a signed-in visitor is counted the same as anyone else.
  • Anything from a third party — no Google Analytics, no hosted Plausible; nothing leaves the VPS.

AOverview

  • Stats is a fourth area on the admin rail new, beside People, Content and Data, and shows to accounts with the new stats permission.
  • One filter row drives every number on the page: a period, and whether to compare it with the period before. The tiles say the change; the charts do not draw a second line.
  • "Active" means signed in to the world, on any app. Being signed in only to the web chat counts too: Community is part of the world, not a separate product.
  • From a visit to a regular joins the two halves: visits → opened World → made an account → signed in to the world → came back within a week. The first two steps are counted visitors, the last three accounts.
  • Every number links to the page that explains it: a tile to World or Web, a place to the map.

BWorld

  • Sessions are the server's own record: a session starts when the handshake is accepted and ends when the connection closes. The app and its version come from the handshake, which carries neither today new.
  • When people are in the world: the number signed in, sampled every minute and averaged per weekday and hour. It answers "when should an event be", and when a deploy hurts least.
  • Who comes back needs to know that the account seen in week 3 is the one that joined in week 1 — the reason sessions are kept per account for 90 days. After that only these percentages remain.
  • What people did counts what passes through the server, by kind. Private messages are counted as "a sealed message was relayed"; the server has no way to count more.
  • Apps in use makes a stale client visible before a person reports it — and refused handshakes show a server older than the app, which today looks like "connection reset".

CWeb

  • Counted by the page, not by Caddy new. The site is one page that changes its address as you move around it, so Caddy only ever sees the first load; the page itself sends POST /api/stats/hit on every change of address, from all three sites.
  • A visitor is one browser on one site on one day. Two days are two visitors: nothing can join them, which is the point. Hence no "returning visitors" here — the world page has that, for people with an account.
  • Where they came from is the previous page's site, never its full address. Search engines are grouped; fediverse servers are kept by name because they say which post worked.
  • Country comes from the IP address at the moment of the request, through a database kept on the VPS (DB-IP Lite, free, no account); the address is then dropped.
  • APK downloads come from Caddy, the one place counted from the log: a download of /apk/cartopolis.apk has no page that could report it.

DPlaces

  • Once a minute the simulator adds a minute to the 250 m square each avatar stands in, and the person to that square's set for the day. The set is reduced to a count at midnight; no point and no track is ever written.
  • Five people or the square stays blank, so a square cannot point at the one person who lives there. The same rule holds for the hour chart in the side pane.
  • Only avatars in the world count. A GPS share is a message between people and is never added here.
  • Places shared and public posts count the points people send to each other and post — useful for seeing where a community lives, not only where people walk.
  • Drawn on the PDOK grey base map; the page would use the map tiles the site already serves.

?Open questions

  • Should a community's moderators see its own numbers? Members active this month, messages per topic, joins and leaves — a Stats tab in the community's settings, counting nothing new.
  • A public "in numbers" block on about.cartopolis.org? Accounts, people active this month, communities, hours in the world. Off until someone turns it on.
  • Can a person see their own activity? Their sessions and devices for the last 90 days, under their account. The law already lets them ask for it; a page saves the email.
  • Per-account sessions for 90 days, or none at all? Without them the counts above still work, but "who comes back", "active in 30 days" and the funnel's last two steps go.
  • Honour Global Privacy Control? Nothing counted can identify anyone, so it is not required; not counting browsers that send it costs a few percent of the visitors.

×Considered and not proposed

  • Self-hosted Plausible or Umami. The same cookie-free counting, but another service and database on a VPS that is already near full, for a few thousand visits a month — and it cannot see the world, which is half of what we want to know.
  • Matomo or Google Analytics. Cookies and a consent banner, and in Google's case every visit leaves for a third party.
  • Reading Caddy's access log for page views. The site changes page without asking Caddy, so the log sees one load per visit and none of the pages after it; and a log puts every IP address on disk.
  • Counting inside the game with a telemetry crate. The client would report on itself; the server already sees every session and relay, and an app cannot lie to it.
