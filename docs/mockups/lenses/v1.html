Lenses — reading the map, apart from how it looks new

The Layers panel has two jobs that fight each other. Most of it says how the world looks: many switches, on or off, set once and left. Figures are something else: what you are reading — one at a time, with a source, a mode (Replay, Exercise, Live) and sometimes a clock. Ticking a switch to open a figure that closes the other one is a radio button dressed as a checkbox; on a phone the panel covers the whole map, so you cannot see what you picked; and the scenario's title card lands on top of the panel header. Scenarios, forecasts and simulations will add dozens more. This sheet moves all of it out of Layers into a lens: its own button, its own sheet that keeps the map visible, and a shelf to choose from.

Layers = how it looks. Lens = what you read.

Layers keeps the switches (ground, names, clouds, hex grid). Everything with a source and a figure moves to the lens. One lens open at a time.

Pick, never tick

A lens shows one figure. The figure list is radio rows, and each row already shows the value here, so you choose by reading, not by trying.

The map stays in view

On a phone the lens is a bottom sheet with three heights: a strip, half, full. You choose with the map visible above your thumb. Landscape and desktop: a side panel.

One place for how real it is

Mode, source and close live in the sheet's header, not in a floating card. The coloured frame round the screen stays, so a screenshot still says Replay or Exercise.

1bHalf — choose a figure

  • Radio rows, not switches: one is on, tapping another moves it. The map above repaints as you tap.
  • Each row shows its own colour ramp and the value at the crosshair, so the list already answers "what is this place like" before you pick.
  • Groups (People, Homes …) come from the scenario file; a lens with forty figures stays a list you can scan.
  • ⋯ holds Sources and About; ✕ closes the lens. Values are illustrative.

1cFull — the shelf of lenses

  • Tapping the lens button with nothing open opens this; with a lens open it opens 1b, and "All lenses" sits at the bottom of the figures.
  • Grouped by the question a person brings (v5-areas: "a question, not a dataset"), never by source or file.
  • Here filters to lenses that have something to say about the place in view (a lens's boards and rungs, MAX_REACH); search covers figure names too.
  • Every card wears its mode, so an exercise never looks like news.

1dTap a place — its card, in the same sheet

  • The place card is the figure list turned round: every figure of the lens for this one place, against its town and the country (compare, never rank).
  • Tapping a row switches the map to that figure — the second way to choose, from a place instead of from a list.
  • "Its town" climbs a rung; "Compare…" picks a second place; "Talk about it" opens the place's chat (v5-areas: every figure leads to people). new

4Decisions in this sheet, and what is open

Decided in the drawing

  • Lens, not layer. The word the scenario sheets already use ("a lens with a clock", v1-engine). One open at a time; opening another replaces it, and the shelf's first card is the one you had.
  • Own button in the top-left column, under Layers. The dock is full at six (dock.rs), and a lens is a control of the view, like Layers.
  • Bottom sheet in portrait, side panel otherwise, with the same three parts. The strip is the resting state; nothing floats over the map except the frame.
  • Radio rows with the value here for the figures; chips for futures and modes inside a lens.
  • Grouped by question on the shelf; lens files gain a question and figure group line in their TOML.

Open

  • Two figures at once? Not here: one lens, one figure. A side-by-side (split screen, ) for comparing two futures or two years is the likely next step, drawn when there are two to compare.
  • The button's icon and name ("Lens", "Figures", "Read") — the word reaches people only through the tooltip and the shelf title.
  • Where a community's own lens goes (a neighbourhood survey, a plan's map): the shelf can hold them under "From your communities"; not drawn.
  • Live stays empty until there is an official feed (NL-Alert has none we know of, scenarios README).
