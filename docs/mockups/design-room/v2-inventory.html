v2: your inventory, and what goes into the shared library

The answers to v1's questions, and the piece they called for: an inventory, after Second Life's. Everything you make or take lives in it, in folders. You place things from it by dragging (desktop) or tapping (phone), and you take things back into it from the world. Moving an item into the shared library needs a new permission that, for now, only admins hold. Everything marked new does not exist today.

Several objectsA design is placed as a group of ordinary objects that remember they belong together. No linked-object wire change in v1.
Drafts on the deviceUnfinished designs never touch the server. They show in the inventory as a Drafts folder, marked "on this device".
The avatar standsIn the room your avatar is a still figure for scale. You don't walk.
An inventoryServer-side and per account, so it follows you to every device. The Models panel's "Mine" list becomes one of its folders.
Sharing is a permissionA new library_share, not in the Player role. Admins hold it, as they hold every permission.

bThe inventory on a phone

  • A full-width panel, like every right panel on a phone. The same tree, with larger rows.
  • Folders you make yourself (long-press ▸ New folder) work as in Places, and use its drag-and-drop tree.
  • The shared library sits under your own folders and is read-only: you take copies from it, you don't move things into it.

dMoving it to the shared library

  • The same licence question uploads ask today, now also asked for designs.
  • A design that uses an uploaded model inherits that model's licence. One "something else" model inside it, and the design cannot be shared.
  • The server checks library_share, the licence and every model inside. The dialog just doesn't offer what would be refused.

eThe inventory inside the room

  • The room's left panel gets a second tab. Drag a model, or a whole object, in from it, and it becomes parts of the design.
  • Bringing in an Object adds a copy of its parts. The original in your inventory doesn't change.

Roles, in the web UI's Admin ▸ People ▸ Roles

PermissionPlayerBuilderCuratorAdmin
prim_create · place objects
library_manage · curate the library
library_share new
move inventory items into the shared library

Not granted to any role at first: only the admin flag carries it, since that holds every permission. Builder and Curator are example roles an operator might make, not built in. Later, granting it to a role is one tick here.

This takes something away: today any signed-in account may share an uploaded model whose licence allows it (the "Share it with everyone" box in the upload dialog). With this, only admins can, and the box disappears for everyone else.

Where each folder lives

FolderStoredIs
Objects newServer, per accountA content document, the same {version, prims} shape /api/content/export already writes, as a new kind of inventory item beside models
Drafts newThis device (platform::storage, Data)The same document, unsent. "Save to inventory" uploads it and it moves to Objects
Models todayServerToday's inventory_items, the Models panel's "Mine"
Trash newServerDeleted items, emptied after 30 days or by hand
Shared library today, extendedServerToday's library_entries (models), now also holding objects

Places stays its own panel: a saved place is a view, not something you put in the world. In Second Life landmarks live in the inventory; here they would just duplicate the Places tree.

Giving an item to another personLater, from a chatSecond Life's drag onto a profile. Here, the natural place is "send" in a private chat. It raises the same licence question as sharing, so it waits for this to settle.
Next-owner rights (copy / modify / transfer)Not in v1These only matter once things change hands. Until then, ownership already decides who may edit (prim_edit_own).
Scripts and textures as itemsLater, as foldersScripts live on objects today, and textures in an operator-managed directory. Both fit the tree once there is a reason to keep one on its own.
Take on something that is not yoursAdmins onlyThe same rule as editing it: an unowned object belongs to the server.
