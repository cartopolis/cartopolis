v3: the design room and the inventory, thought through

v2 redrawn around three answers (everything in the inventory lives on the server, no isometric views, sharing stays open to everyone), plus a second pass over the whole design that changed four more things. The room is now drawn in perspective, the way the app would actually show it. Everything marked new does not exist today.

1Everything is on the server, drafts tooThe room saves the draft every few seconds after an edit. A stall started on the phone on the square can be finished on the desktop at home. So Design here needs to be signed in, as Build does.v2: drafts on the device
2Perspective only, with views as places to standThe room uses the map's own orbit camera. Instead of Front / Side / Top it offers Orbit, Eye level and From the street: standing on the far side of the real kerb, at 1.65 m, looking at it the way a passer-by will.v1: Around / Front / Side / Top
3Two ways to give to everyoneShare stays what it is today, for anyone: a copy in the library, still yours, credited, and you can take it back. Move to the server library is new and admin-only: the server takes the item over, and the maker can't withdraw it any more.v2: one share act, admin-only, which took today's sharing away
4Placing makes a copyAs in Second Life: an inventory item works like a stamp, and what stands in the world is separate from it. Take makes a new item. Design on something placed edits that thing, and the room's button then reads Update in the world.v2: items marked "in world", linked to their copies
5A group does need the wireCorrecting v2: for one tap to select the whole stall, every client has to know which objects belong together. That means a group field on each object, a protocol revision and a column. A group still doesn't move as one piece on its own; editing it moves every member.v2: "no wire change"
6Others can see you are designingWhile you are in the room your avatar stays on the map, drawn pale with a soft glow and labelled "designing", so nobody talks to a frozen figure and wonders why it doesn't answer. It says that you are designing, not what.new

What it takes, against what exists

PieceBuilt on
ObjectsThe {version, prims} document /api/content/export writes today, stored like a model: a file named by its own hash. That keeps sharing and the library per file, as they are now. new kind
DraftsThe same document, but in a row that changes on every save. It only becomes a hashed file when saved to Objects or placed, so autosave doesn't leave a trail of files. new
FoldersOne folder on today's inventory_items, plus a table of the folders people make. Models move in unchanged. new
Place/api/content/import, which already creates objects owned by the caller. It gains a spot to place at and a group id. today, extended
GroupA group on each object, sent to every client: one protocol revision. wire
TakeExport the group as a document, then delete it: two existing steps, done as one. new route
DesigningA presence bit beside the existing away state. wire
Server librarylibrary_entries gains shelf (server | people). A moved item's owner is cleared, as for a content pack. new
Room cameraThe orbit camera, plus two fixed standing points. No orthographic camera.

Designing togetherLater, but keep it possibleWith the draft on the server, inviting a second person into your room is "two clients editing one draft". Worth doing once the room exists; nothing here should rule it out.
How big can a design be?64 objects, 20 m acrossA bound per design, so one Place can't flood a square. A cap per account in the world is a separate question for the server.
Can a curator move someone else's shared item to the server shelf?Only with the maker's yesIt changes ownership. The maker gets a notice ("Move Market stall to the server library?") and the move waits for their answer.
Giving an item to one personLater, from a private chatThe licence question is the same as for sharing, so it fits once both kinds of giving are settled.
Next-owner rights (copy / modify / transfer)Not in v1Every copy is fully yours. Rights only matter once things change hands between people.
