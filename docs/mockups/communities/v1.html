Communities: one idea instead of rooms, workspaces and channels

A design proposal, not code. Everything in a community is new. Today the app has rooms (unencrypted, join by typing a name) and the web has workspaces → public and private channels → asking to join. This replaces all of that with one rule: being in a community means being in every one of its topics. One-to-one chats, groups, Here and Everyone stay exactly as they are.

A person

A one-to-one chat. Encrypted. Unchanged.

A group

A private chat with people you pick. Encrypted. Only its members know it exists. Unchanged.

A community

A named set of people with a few topics. Join it and you are in all of them. Encrypted; the server knows the names, not what is said.

Here · Everyone

The world's public chats: whoever is near you, and the whole server. Not encrypted, by nature. Unchanged.

What goes, what stays

TodayAfter
Rooms appGone. A public topic is a community; a public place is Here.
Workspaces webCommunities, on the web and the app alike.
Public channelsTopics. Everyone in the community is in all of them.
Private channelsGone. Something private is an ordinary group with those people.
"Ask to join a channel"Gone. You are in a community or you are not.
Owner / admin / memberThe person who started it, plus whoever they let add people. Everyone may start a topic.
One-to-one chats, groupsunchanged
Here, Everyoneunchanged
App knows nothing of workspacesThe app shows communities like screen A; today it shows a channel as a bare group.

Open questions

  • Can somebody find and join a community themselves (a public directory or an invite link), or only by being added? This draws "only by being added", the simplest.
  • Is "topic" the right word? Alternatives: "channel" (familiar, but it is what is being retired) or "thread" (taken: replies already use it).
  • A big community puts every member in every topic. Fine at tens of people; at hundreds the encryption gets slower to update, and that is when a public directory would want a different design.
