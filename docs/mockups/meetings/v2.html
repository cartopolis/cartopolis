Meetings: the golden standard, on calls nobody else can open

Version 2 of the meeting design. It keeps v1's tools and changes how they look and behave. Calls stay what docs/notes/calls.md makes them: end-to-end encrypted, forwarded by a server that cannot open a frame. A meeting still belongs to a chat with a place in the world (../workspace/), and Room is still one of its layouts. Nothing new happens in the world; this is about the conferencing itself. Everything is NEW unless marked TODAY.

The stage is the meetingFaces and the shared screen get the space. Controls float on glass over it, and panels are separate islands, never a frame around it.
Act where you are toldA knock, a hand, a recording: each arrives as a notice with its action in it. Admit from the notice, without opening a panel.
State is never hiddenMuted, recording, presenting, encrypted, reconnecting: always on screen, always in words as well as colour.
Catch the mistakesTalking while muted, leaving as the last host, sharing a screen with notifications: the meeting notices and offers the fix.
Keys and thumbsEvery desktop action has a key, shown in its tooltip. On the phone every action is in reach of a thumb.
Private by constructionEach tool runs where the encryption allows (section 8), and the meeting says so instead of asking for trust.

Recording kept on Sanne's device

The record is an ordinary sealed message. Its to-dos still tick afterwards, so it is also where the follow-up happens. The transcript is everyone's captions joined up. A recording stays where it was made until its maker sends it, like any file.

Why it is all chat messages

  • The chat is already end-to-end encrypted and already reaches every member's devices, including those who missed the meeting. Notes, polls, hands, reactions, spotlight and the record are new kinds of sealed chat message, not new server features.
  • So the server's view of a meeting is no more than what it already sees of a call: who joined and left, and when.
  • A meeting with guests gets a short-lived group of its own. Its record is posted back to the home chat, which the guests are not in.
  • Slash commands in Notes: /todo /decided /poll /timer, so a note-taker never reaches for the mouse.

7Order of work

Three rounds, each usable on its own.

1 · A meeting that holds together

Every camera sent in a large and a small size, with receivers asking only for what they show. Then the new layout of the stage and the dock, Speaker and Gallery with who-is-talking, screen sharing (browser and desktop), hands, reactions, notices, the muted nudge, hosts with mute, remove, lock and the waiting room, hiding background noise, and the bad-connection states.

2 · Before and after

Planning a meeting and its card in the chat, reminders and the .ics file, the check before joining with its network test, guests by link, the agenda with its timer, shared notes, polls, and the meeting record.

3 · The heavy ones, on the device

Captions and the transcript, blurring the background, recording, breakout rooms, and screen sharing from Android. Each one starts by measuring its cost on the phone.

8Where each tool runs, and what it costs

The server forwards sealed frames and keeps the roster (docs/notes/calls.md). Each tool below is either something the server enforces with what it already sees (who is connected, which stream is whose), or something the devices do inside the encryption.

Lobby, admit, deny, lock, removeServer enforcesWho is waiting (it already sees who joins)A waiting state before CallJoined; it already refuses a blocked person before any media flows. Removing someone also removes them from the chat's encryption group, so the key changes.
Host mute, “may not unmute / share”Server enforcesThat a stream is held backThe forwarder drops that person's audio or screen stream. Roles (host, co-host) go in the roster.
Screen sharingSharer's deviceThat a third stream existsA stream kind beyond CallTrack.video: bool (a protocol revision). Browser: getDisplayMedia. Desktop app: a capture library per OS. Encoded at up to 1280×720 but only 1–5 pictures a second, which a software VP9 encoder can manage (640×480 took 102 ms a picture).

Captions and transcriptEach speaker's own deviceNothingSpeech recognition on the device that hears the voice first and clearest, sent as sealed text. Needs a local speech model (Whisper-class, 40–150 MB) and its CPU cost on a phone is unmeasured. Browsers' built-in speech recognition sends the audio to Google or Apple, so it is not used.
Blur backgroundSender's deviceNothingA person-segmentation model on every picture before encoding. Costs CPU on the same thread as the encoder; desktop first.
RecordingOne person's deviceNothing, until they share the fileThe recorder's device mixes what it already receives. Browser: MediaRecorder. Allowed only if the meeting allows it, and announced to everyone with a sound and the red chip.
Guests by linkServer and host's deviceA guest session with a name, no accountThe server has no account-less sessions today, and opens a chat only between contacts. The link carries a one-time token; the guest's browser makes a temporary key, and the host's device adds it to the meeting's group on Admit.
Breakout roomsServer routes, devices keyWho is in which roomThe forwarder sends each room's streams only within the room. Keys: section 9.
Dial in by phoneNot offered. A telephone gateway would have to decrypt the call, which is the one thing the server must never be able to do.

9To decide

Breakout rooms: one key or one per room?

Drawn: one key, and the server keeps the rooms apart by forwarding each room's streams only inside it. Cheap and instant, but anyone in the meeting could hear another room if the server sent it. The alternative, a separate encryption group per room, takes a few seconds per room to set up.

Guests without an account?

Drawn: yes, by a link, always through the lobby, with a name only. It is the one tool that needs the server to accept someone it has no account for, and the first thing a work meeting with outsiders needs.

Recording at all?

Drawn: on a device only, off by default, allowed per meeting, and announced. Nobody can stop someone pointing a second phone at the screen, so this is about making it visible and honest, not about preventing it.

Captions: worth a model download?

Drawn: yes, on demand: the model downloads the first time someone turns captions on. Languages: Dutch and English at first. Whether a phone can keep up is the first thing to measure.

Who hosts in a community?

Drawn: whoever planned or started the meeting, plus anyone they make co-host. In a community, should its moderators always be hosts too?

How much of the world, now?

Drawn: what ../workspace/ already has, and no more. A meeting has the chat's place, Room is one of the four layouts, and walking into the place joins the call. Hearing by distance and Share my view stay in that sheet and are not part of these rounds.
