Calls: the tools a professional meeting needs

A design proposal, not code. Today a call can ring, be answered, mute, turn the camera on and off, fold to a bar and end (systems::call_screen, src/lib/call.ts). This sheet adds what a work meeting expects from Zoom, Teams or Meet: scheduling, a lobby, hosts, screen sharing, raised hands, captions, notes and a record afterwards. Everything is drawn so it still holds under the rule calls already live by: the server forwards sealed frames and must never be able to open one. So every tool says where it runs. It builds on ../workspace/ v1, sheet 2: a call belongs to a chat that has a place in the world, and Room (everyone's avatar in that place) stays one of the layouts. Nothing here adds more to do in the world; the new tools are the ordinary conferencing ones. Drawn in the web UI's tokens (src/css/theme.css, dark half) and the app's call keys (theme::call_button). Everything here is NEW except what is marked TODAY.

The top line

  • Title, chat, timer and Encrypted as today's call header has them TODAY.
  • The agenda item in a chip with the time it has left. It turns gold at one minute and red when over; the host moves on with one click in the Agenda tab.
  • Recording names whose device records. The server cannot record (it has no key), so a recording only ever exists on someone's own device, and everybody sees that it does.
  • Four layouts. Speaker (who talks is big), Gallery (everyone equal), Shared screen (picked automatically when somebody shares), and Room from ../workspace/, where the call takes place in the chat's place. Each person picks their own; a host's Spotlight overrides it for everyone.
  • The place is in the header, and the strip's last tile is a live look into it: four people joined from the world, by walking into the courtyard.

The stage

  • A shared screen fills the stage, with the speakers in a strip. The presenter's pointer shows as a ring, as in ../workspace/'s Share my view.
  • Whoever talks gets a teal ring, worked out on each device from the sound it decodes, so the server learns nothing new.
  • Hand numbers on the pictures match the queue in the panel. Daan blurred his background.
  • Captions at the bottom, with the speaker's name. Each speaker's own device writes them (section 7).

The keys

  • Today's round keys and labels TODAY, plus Share, Hand, React, Captions and More. The small arrow on Mic and Camera picks the device. The arrow on Leave offers End for everyone to a host.

The People tab

  • Lobby first, because someone is waiting. A guest is marked as one.
  • Hands in the order they went up; Let talk lowers the hand and lets a muted person unmute.
  • A host's ⋯ on a person: mute, stop camera, spotlight, co-host, back to the lobby, remove.

The meeting record, an ordinary sealed message in the chat. The checkboxes still work afterwards, so To do is also where the actions get ticked off. The transcript is the captions joined up, if anyone had them on. A recording stays where it was made; sending it to the chat is the recorder's choice, as any file is.

Why it is all chat messages

  • The chat is already end-to-end encrypted and already reaches every member's devices, including the ones that were not in the call. Notes, polls, hands, reactions, spotlight and the record are new kinds of sealed chat messages, not new server features.
  • So the server's view of a meeting is what it already sees for a call: who joined and left, and when.
  • A meeting with guests gets its own short-lived group with the members in it, and the record is posted back to the home chat without the guests in it.

7Where each tool runs, and what it costs

The server forwards sealed frames and keeps the roster (docs/notes/calls.md). Each tool below is either something the server enforces with what it already sees (who is connected, which stream is whose), or something the devices do inside the encryption.

ToolRuns onThe server learnsWhat it takes
Lobby, admit, deny, lock, removeServer enforcesWho is waiting (it already sees who joins)A waiting state before CallJoined; it already refuses a blocked person before any media flows. Removing someone also removes them from the chat's encryption group, so the key changes.
Host mute, “may not unmute / share”Server enforcesThat a stream is held backThe forwarder drops that person's audio or screen stream. Roles (host, co-host) go in the roster.
Screen sharingSharer's deviceThat a third stream existsA stream kind beyond CallTrack.video: bool (a protocol revision). Browser: getDisplayMedia. Desktop app: a capture library per OS. Encoded at up to 1280×720 but only 1–5 pictures a second, which a software VP9 encoder can manage (640×480 took 102 ms a picture).
Big meetings (9+ cameras)Server and devicesWhich streams each person watchesEach sender sends a large and a small picture, and receivers ask only for the tiles they show. Today every picture goes to everyone at full size, and the forwarder does no congestion control yet. This comes before Gallery, or a phone in a nine-person call drowns.
Who is talking, layouts, pinEach deviceNothingSound level from audio already decoded; layouts are the web panel's grid code and call_screen's tiles.
Hands, reactions, spotlight, notes, agenda, polls, the record, Going / MaybeDevices, as sealed chat messagesThat messages were sentNew message kinds inside the encrypted chat (cartopolis_chat), so the app and the web page get them together.
Hide background noiseSender's deviceNothingAn RNNoise filter before Opus. A pure-Rust port exists (nnnoiseless); browsers have their own noiseSuppression.
Captions and transcriptEach speaker's own deviceNothingSpeech recognition on the device that hears the voice first and clearest, sent as sealed text. Needs a local speech model (Whisper-class, 40–150 MB) and its CPU cost on a phone is unmeasured. Browsers' built-in speech recognition sends the audio to Google or Apple, so it is not used.
Blur backgroundSender's deviceNothingA person-segmentation model on every picture before encoding. Costs CPU on the same thread as the encoder; desktop first.
RecordingOne person's deviceNothing, until they share the fileThe recorder's device mixes what it already receives. Browser: MediaRecorder. Allowed only if the meeting allows it, and announced to everyone with a sound and the red chip.
Guests by linkServer and host's deviceA guest session with a name, no accountThe server has no account-less sessions today, and opens a chat only between contacts. The link carries a one-time token; the guest's browser makes a temporary key, and the host's device adds it to the meeting's group on Admit.
Breakout roomsServer routes, devices keyWho is in which roomThe forwarder sends each room's streams only within the room. Keys: section 9.
Dial in by phoneNot offered. A telephone gateway would have to decrypt the call, which is the one thing the server must never be able to do.

8Order of work

Three rounds. Each one is usable on its own.

1 · A meeting that holds together

Small and large pictures, and receivers asking only for what they show. Then Speaker and Gallery layouts with who-is-talking, screen sharing (browser and desktop), hands and reactions, the call chat tab, hosts with mute, remove, lock and the lobby, and hiding background noise.

2 · Before and after

Planning a meeting and its card in the chat, the reminder and the .ics, the check-before-joining page, guests by link, the agenda with its timer, shared notes, polls, and the meeting record.

3 · The heavy ones, on the device

Captions and the transcript, blurring the background, recording, breakout rooms, and screen sharing from Android. Each one starts by measuring the cost on the phone.

9To decide

Breakout rooms: one key or one per room?

Drawn: one key, and the server keeps the rooms apart by forwarding each room's streams only inside it. Cheap and instant, but anyone in the meeting could hear another room if the server sent it. The alternative, a separate encryption group per room, takes a few seconds per room to set up.

Guests without an account?

Drawn: yes, by a link, always through the lobby, with a name only. It is the one tool that needs the server to accept someone it has no account for, and the first thing a work meeting with outsiders needs.

Recording at all?

Drawn: on a device only, off by default, allowed per meeting, and announced. Nobody can stop someone pointing a second phone at the screen, so this is about making it visible and honest, not about preventing it.

Captions: worth a model download?

Drawn: yes, on demand: the model downloads the first time someone turns captions on. Languages: Dutch and English at first. Whether a phone can keep up is the first thing to measure.

Who hosts in a community?

Drawn: whoever planned or started the meeting, plus anyone they make co-host. In a community, should its moderators always be hosts too?

How much of the world, now?

Drawn: what ../workspace/ already has, and no more. A meeting has the chat's place, Room is one of the four layouts, and walking into the place joins the call. Hearing by distance and Share my view stay in that sheet and are not part of these rounds.
