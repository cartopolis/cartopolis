The macro view as a strategy map new

Above about 40 km the 3D map crossfades into a painted, flat map in the manner of a strategy game's strategic view: clear land cover, roads with a hierarchy, cities as banners, borders that glow. It is the surface the macro features hang off — lenses, scenarios, communities by region. Every map on this sheet is drawn from real data: ESA WorldCover 2021 from our own tile server (z6), Natural Earth roads, rail, rivers and borders, and OSM city populations.

Read at a glance

Cities named and sized, motorways in one warm colour, land cover in eight distinct classes instead of two greens.

A crossfade, not a mode

The painted map fades in on altitude (space_factor), the 3D city fades out. Zooming back in lands where you looked.

Lenses

One rail of lenses re-colours the same map: terrain, built-up, mobility, communities, weather, scenarios.

Scenarios

A scenario is a lens with a clock: it plays a modelled event over the map. The first is a nuclear exchange (other sheet).

3Lenses re-colour the same map new

Each is one hex grid (about 9 km across here) classified from the same data. Hexes are the unit the macro features aggregate to: a lens, a scenario's damage, a community's reach.

Terrain. The majority WorldCover class of each hex: farmland gold, grassland green, forest dark with trees, built-up grey-brown. The Veluwe, the Drenthe forests and the Eifel read immediately.
Built-up. Share of each hex that is residential, from pale to orange. The Randstad, Brabant's city chain and the Ruhr light up; the north stays dark.
Mobility. Motorways in gold, other main roads in grey, railways in teal. Natural Earth's network at this scale; OSM's would follow as you zoom in.

Where the data comes from

  • Land cover: ESA WorldCover 2021 (CC BY 4.0), tiles.cartopolis.org/tiles/landcover at z6.
  • Roads, railways, rivers, lakes, borders: Natural Earth 1:10m.
  • City populations: OSM place=city from overpass.cartopolis.org; Belgian and German cities from Natural Earth.
  • Drawn by gen.ts in this folder.

Found while drawing it

  • The landcover archive holds no forest over the Netherlands from z7 up (z6 has 930 forest polygons over this box, z7–z10 none); Germany has it at every zoom. If the client reads z7+ here, the Veluwe is drawn as grass, which may be part of why the view looks flat.
