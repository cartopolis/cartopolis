Scenario lens: a nuclear exchange new

A scenario plays a modelled event over the strategy map with a clock you can scrub. This one follows Plan A (Princeton Program on Science and Global Security, 2019): a NATO–Russia war that turns nuclear in Europe and escalates in three phases. Zoomed out it shows the exchange; zoomed in, what one detonation does to the ground under it and where its fallout goes. The scenario's figures are the study's; the effects are standard weapon-effects scaling; the map is real data.

Sober, not a game

No score, no "win", no choosing targets. You watch a published model play out; every number names its source.

Asked for, then warned

Opened from the Scenarios lens only, behind a one-line content note. Never shown to anyone who did not open it.

One clock, every scale

The timeline is shared: Europe shows the arcs, a country the rings and plumes, a town the blast circles.

The map stays real

Blast rings and plumes are drawn on the same land cover, roads and towns as everything else.

Sources and assumptions

  • Scenario, phases and casualty figures: Princeton SGS, Plan A (2019), which used NUKEMAP and published force postures.
  • Bases: the six NATO nuclear-sharing bases named in public reporting (Volkel, Kleine Brogel, Büchel, Aviano, Ghedi, Incirlik).
  • Rings: Glasstone & Dolan scaling for a 100 kt surface burst, rounded. Plumes: simplified ellipses of the WSEG-10 kind, for shape and order of magnitude only.
  • Towns inside the plumes are computed (OSM place=city); in this wind only Venlo falls inside a plume, Kleine Brogel's 10 rad/h one.

What a real version needs

  • A population grid (CBS 100 m, GHSL elsewhere) so a ring counts people instead of naming towns.
  • The day's real wind from the weather lens, so the plume is the one that would fall today.
  • An "after" phase: the months of fallout decay, and the soot-and-cooling studies for a global view.
  • The same scenario frame for gentler models: a river flood, a heat wave, a storm surge on the Dutch coast.
