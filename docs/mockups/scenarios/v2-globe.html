From the macro map to the globe: one hex board new

Today's hex layer is a single grid of ~9 km cells laid in Web Mercator (hex_grid.wesl), drawn between ~100 and ~4,000 km. Zoom out further and the cells shrink to specks; Mercator also stretches them towards the poles, and a flat grid cannot close round a sphere. The proposal: a grid made for the whole Earth (H3), whose cells merge seven into one each time you climb about 2.6 times higher. A cell then stays 30–70 px across at every altitude, from the macro map to the whole globe, like the tiles of a strategy game's world map. The same cells are where the scenario engine's fields and counters are added up.

Same tile size at every height

The resolution follows altitude, so a cell is always about a finger's width on screen: readable, clickable, never noise.

No jump

Over a band of altitude the seven small cells fade out as their parent fades in, on space_factor-style ramps, like every other altitude effect.

Values roll up

A parent holds its children's sum (people, check-ins), worst value and share (danger), or majority (land cover). Nothing is recomputed per zoom.

A board that closes

A sphere of hexes needs 12 pentagons. H3 puts all twelve at sea, so every land tile is a hexagon.

Why H3, and what it brings

  • A hex grid over the whole sphere, from Uber, open source (Apache 2.0), with a pure-Rust port (h3o) that builds for wasm and Android.
  • Each cell has a 64-bit id; its parent and children are arithmetic on the id. The scenario engine, atlas and the simulator can all key their numbers by cell.
  • Cells are near-equal in area, so a count per cell compares across Europe; Mercator's cells do not.
  • Seven children do not tile their parent exactly; the edge cells straddle. For counts this is handled by assigning each child to one parent, which H3 defines.

What a real version needs

  • Drawing the cells on the globe tiles in the ground shader (a cell id per fragment), so the lines follow the terrain and the night side like today's grid.
  • A per-cell value texture for the lens, filled from the rolled-up numbers; the realistic globe stays underneath.
  • The existing hex layer setting kept: off by default, now meaning this grid at every height.
  • A population grid (GHSL) so cells can count people at every level.
