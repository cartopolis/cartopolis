One whole: every scale, every mode new

The earlier sheets drew pieces: the strategy map, the engine, a nuclear exchange, a night of drones, a phone, the hex globe, the stars. This one puts them together. There is one ladder of places from your street to the nearest stars; each rung is a board (cells or a graph); a simulation runs at the rung where it is native and is read at every other; one clock and one "where you are" card go with you up and down; and four modes, each with its own frame, say how real what you see is. Below: the ladder, two scenarios followed through it, how the parts stack, and which mode belongs where.

One ladder of places

Street, city, region, country, continent, world, solar system, stars. A place on one rung is a cell or a node; opening it shows the rung below.

Native scale, read everywhere

A fallout model runs at kilometres, a solar storm at the Sun. Rolling up and drilling down give each a reading at every other rung.

One clock, one "you"

The time bar and your place card stay on screen at every height, so the thread from "a wave over Poland" to "your hallway" is never lost.

3How the parts stack

PeopleCommunities at every rung: housemates, the street, the city, a country, the world — and a colony in the sandbox. They check in, ask for and offer help, and run exercises together.
ReadingsWhat a scenario means for a place, in plain words: rolled up for the rungs above (sum, worst and share, majority), drilled down for the rungs below, and the "you" card on every screen.
ScenarioEight primitives on a clock (v1-engine), each with a source and a confidence, placed on a board.
ModelsNever ours alone: a study (Plan A), a service (KNMI, LIWO's flood scenarios, NOAA's and ESA's space weather), an official alert, or our own simple model labelled as such.
BoardsCells (H3, v2) for what spreads over ground; graphs (v3) for what lives in places and moves along routes — roads, grids, flights, planets, stars.
The real mapWhat Cartopolis already is: the 3D city, the painted macro map, the globe, the people in it. Every simulation is drawn on it, never instead of it.

Solid: the mode is at home there. Faint: possible, rarely the point. The sandbox stays off Earth so fiction can never be mistaken for the real map; a game on Earth would be a separate world, not a layer.

Rules that make it one whole

  • Every primitive is placed on a board at its native rung, and every rung can read it.
  • The clock and the "you" card never disappear while a scenario is open.
  • The frame of the mode is on every screen and every rung; the red frame belongs to an official source only.
  • Zooming never changes mode; leaving a scenario is one button, always in the same place.
  • People are part of every scenario: a reading that can be acted on together ("who needs help") beats one that only informs.

A build order that stays useful at each step

  • 1. The scenario crate with region states and tracks, played as the drone exercise on the macro map (region and country).
  • 2. The H3 cell board with roll-up: the fallout field, and the hex globe replacing today's Mercator grid.
  • 3. Drill-down to the street: the "you" card and readings on the 3D ground.
  • 4. Live: one official feed end to end (KNMI warnings are public), then others.
  • 5. The graph board: the power grid on Earth, then space weather from the Sun.
  • 6. The sandbox world among the stars.
