Figures about places — and where people use them new

The planning offices (SCP, CPB, PBL) and CBS describe the Netherlands area by area: who lives where, how people live, what the future may bring. In Cartopolis this is not a separate statistics app. It uses the same system as the simulations: the figures are region states and counters on a third kind of board — official areas (neighbourhood → district → municipality → province → country), beside cells and graphs; a forecast is a scenario clock that runs into the future. And it answers the UX question first: who looks at this, where, and to do what?

A question, not a dataset

People ask "how is my neighbourhood doing?" or "where do older people live alone?", not for table 85984NED. The question picks the lens.

Compare, never rank

A neighbourhood is shown against its town and the country, never as "worst of 13,000". No single score on a place.

Small numbers stay hidden

What CBS hides to protect people stays hidden; a neighbourhood under 50 people is grey.

Then do something

Every figure leads to people: the communities and events that work on it here, or a meeting to talk about it.

Who it is for

  • Residents: understand their own place, find others who care about the same thing.
  • Community organisers: see where the people they want to reach live, and what is already going on there.
  • Councillors, journalists, students: the figures with their sources one tap away, and the futures that are on the table.

Where the figures come from

  • CBS: Kerncijfers wijken en buurten (yearly, per neighbourhood), and its 100 m / 500 m square statistics. Drawn here from PDOK's wijkenbuurten 2024 service.
  • PBL with CBS: the regional population and household forecasts per municipality to 2050.
  • CPB with PBL: the WLO scenarios for welfare and the living environment — two or more futures, some regionalised.
  • SCP: reports on how people live (the Sociale Staat van Nederland and others). Mostly national; they explain a lens rather than colour it, and link from the card.
  • Health and loneliness per municipality and district: the GGD/RIVM Gezondheidsmonitor.

Choices and open questions

  • No income or "status" lens by default: it ranks neighbourhoods and can stigmatise them. Available behind a question, always compared, never coloured red.
  • Areas change every year; a time bar over ten years needs CBS's figures recalculated to one year's boundaries, or cells.
  • Drawn by gen.ts (ONLY=areas,apd); classes and averages computed from the same data (the national figures weighted by residents, households or homes).
