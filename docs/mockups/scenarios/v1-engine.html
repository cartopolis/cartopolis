Scenarios: one engine, many simulations new

A scenario is a lens with a clock. It knows nothing about bombs, drones or floods: it plays a timeline of a few primitives — a point, rings, a plume, a track, a region's state, an arc, a field, a counter — over the real map, at every zoom. A model (a published study, a weather run, a flood model, a live feed) produces the primitives; Cartopolis only draws them, says where they came from, and turns them into what it means for a place. A nuclear exchange and a drone war are the first two; a dike breach, a storm, a heat wave or a power cut use the same parts.

The model is not ours

Every scenario names its model and its sources. Cartopolis draws and explains; it does not invent figures.

Uncertainty is drawn

Every primitive carries how sure it is: a track's cone widens, a plume's edge is soft, an invented position is marked.

A simulation never looks real

Replay and exercise wear their label on every screen; only Live, fed by an official source, may look like a warning.

About places, not targets

Results go to regions and hexes — who should shelter, where help is needed — never to picking what to hit.

1Eight primitives

Each has a place, a time window, a source and a confidence. Far out they aggregate to hexes and regions; close in they lie on the ground as a GroundLayer like any other overlay.

Point event

Something happened here, then: a detonation, a dike breach, an outage's origin.

nuclear · flood · outage · fire

Rings

Radii from a point, each with a meaning: blast pressure, an evacuation zone, a cordon.

nuclear · chemical · cordon

Plume

A source carried by the day's wind into a field that grows and decays over hours.

fallout · chemical · smoke · ash

Track

A moving thing or group: where it was, where it is, a cone of where it may be, times along it.

drones · storm · evacuation · convoy

Region state

A province, municipality or safety region coloured by a state with a time: threat now, soon, later, passed.

air alert · flood warning · outage

Arc

A link between two places: a launch and its arrival, a flow of people, help sent.

missiles · migration · supply

Field

A value over the ground, per hex far out and per cell close in: dose, water depth, temperature, smoke.

fallout · flood · heat · air quality

Counter

A number on a place: people affected, shelters free, check-ins, beds. Rolls up from hexes to regions to countries.

casualties · shelters · check-ins

2Three ways to run a scenario

Replay

A published model or a past event

Plays a study's timeline as written, or what happened on a past night, with every figure from its source.

  • Plan A, a NATO–Russia nuclear exchange (Princeton SGS, 2019)
  • The drones over Poland, night of 9–10 September 2025
  • The 1953 North Sea flood on today's map
Exercise

What if, here, today?

The same models with your own place and today's real wind and weather. Striped and labelled on every screen, never sent to anyone as an alert.

  • "Where would fallout from Volkel go in today's wind?"
  • A drone wave over a night, for a municipality's drill
  • Practice your household's plan
Live

Official feeds only

Shows what the authorities publish, on the map, with what it means where you are. Cartopolis makes no warning of its own, ever.

  • NL-Alert, per area, as published
  • KNMI weather warnings, RIVM's radiation monitoring network
  • Elsewhere, each country's official alert service

The same parts, other simulations

  • Dike breach: point, then a water-depth field that spreads by the hour, evacuation tracks, shelters as counters.
  • Storm: a track with its cone, a wind field, region states from KNMI codes.
  • Heat wave: a temperature field over the built-up lens; counters of cool rooms open.
  • Power cut: region states spreading along the grid, counters of homes without power.
  • Chemical fire: a point, a smoke plume in today's wind, "windows shut" regions.

What a real version needs

  • A crate for the scenario types and "state at time t", with no Bevy, so atlas, the simulator and the client share it.
  • A population grid (CBS 100 m in the Netherlands, GHSL elsewhere) so counters count people.
  • The day's wind from a forecast for plumes and tracks; the client already has the real sun.
  • An adapter per official feed for Live, and nothing in Live that the feed did not say.
  • A content note before any war scenario opens, as v1-nuclear proposed.
