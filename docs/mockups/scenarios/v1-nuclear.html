Scenario: a nuclear exchange, the whole continent new

macro-view's v1-nuclear showed the first hours: arcs across Europe and the rings around one base. This is the macro picture that follows: two days on, where the fallout has come down, drawn as a field of hexes over every country at once, and what that means for the one place you care about. The exchange follows Plan A (Princeton SGS, 2019) as before; the fallout is our own simple model and says so.

The question is "where to shelter"

The field's bands are the decisions a person faces — stay indoors, shelter properly, leave — not damage scores.

The wind decides

The same strikes put fallout over different countries on different days. Exercise mode replays it in today's forecast wind.

Your place first

A card for where you are (or a place you choose) says, in plain words, what this scenario would mean there.

Opened on purpose

2After: years, the whole planet

The end of the time bar opens a globe. Not drawn here; it would use the painted globe and its night side from macro-view v3.

What the globe would show

  • Soot from burning cities spreading round the hemisphere in weeks, from the studies of nuclear winter (Robock, Toon and colleagues).
  • Harvests failing: Xia et al. (Nature Food, 2022) estimate more than 5 billion deaths from famine after a full US–Russia war.
  • A counter per country of the studied calorie loss, as a field over farmland from the land-cover lens.

Sources and assumptions

  • Exchange: Princeton SGS, Plan A (2019). Bases: the publicly named nuclear-sharing bases.
  • Fallout: a power law along the wind, fitted to v1-nuclear's 100 kt contours (1000 / 100 / 10 / 1 rad/h at 9 / 32 / 80 / 150 km, about five times that over 48 hours), width growing with distance, scaled by yield. No terrain, no rain, one wind — for shape and order of magnitude only.
  • Strikes in the east: illustrative positions, not targets.
  • Drawn by gen.ts in this folder; the map is Natural Earth.
