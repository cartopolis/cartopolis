Beyond the globe: nodes and lanes, as in Master of Orion new

Climb past the hex globe of v2 and the Earth becomes one node. Above that the board is no longer cells but a graph: places as nodes, the routes between them as lanes, the way Master of Orion connects star systems. The solar system is a handful of nodes; the stellar neighbourhood is every real star system within 16 light years, from the HYG catalogue, joined to its nearest neighbours. The scenario engine needs only one new thing for this: a second kind of board. The same graph works on Earth too, for cities and the rail, air and power links between them.

Two kinds of board

Cells (H3) for anything spread over ground; graphs (nodes and lanes) for anything that lives in places and moves along routes.

Zoom goes up a level

A node opens into the board below it: a star into its system, a planet into its hex globe, the globe into the macro map, down to the street.

Real first

The stars, their distances and their known planets are real data. Anything invented — a colony, a fleet — lives in a sandbox world marked as fiction.

Same primitives

A track runs along lanes, a region state colours nodes, a counter sits on a node. Nothing in the engine knows it is in space.

2The stellar neighbourhood as a board

45 systems from HYG v4.1 (stars within 0.15 pc of a brighter one count as one system), drawn above the plane of Sol's equator with a stalk to it, as star maps do. Each system has a lane to its nearest neighbour and to up to two more within 9.5 light years. Green dots: planets listed in the NASA Exoplanet Archive (a selection). The sandbox colony and the numbers in the panel are invented.

Sources and how it is drawn

  • Stars: the HYG database v4.1 (Astronexus, CC BY-SA 4.0), distances under 4.9 pc (16 ly), positions in its equatorial x, y, z.
  • Lanes: nearest neighbour always, plus up to two more under 2.9 pc. A design choice for readability, not physics.
  • Planets: a selection of hosts from the NASA Exoplanet Archive; the dot count is only indicative.
  • The solar system: real order of the planets, log-scaled distances. Drawn by gen-stars.ts.

What it would need

  • A graph board type in the scenario crate beside the cell board: nodes with an id and a place, lanes with a length.
  • The camera's ladder extended past the globe, each level a crossfade, never a separate mode.
  • A rule kept from the start: the real map never shows fiction; a sandbox world is entered on purpose and wears its label.
  • A decision on what the space layer is for: a game, a teaching tool, or a place for communities to imagine together.
