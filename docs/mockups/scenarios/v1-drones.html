Scenario: a night of drones over Europe new

A hypothetical night in which waves of slow one-way attack drones cross from Belarus and western Russia into the Baltic states, Poland and further west — the pattern Ukraine has lived with every night since 2022, moved onto the EU's map. It uses two primitives: tracks (groups of drones with a cone of where they may go) and region states (each province coloured by when the nearest group would reach it). That second one is what an air-alert map for citizens is.

Regions, not aim points

Tracks end in widening cones and regions light up; the map never says what a drone is aimed at.

Time is the message

Every region says how long it has. "Under an hour" matters to a person more than a position does.

An exercise looks like one

Striped edge, "exercise" in the top bar, invented numbers marked. Only an official feed may drop the stripes.

Replays of real nights

The same frame replays a documented night — such as the drones that crossed into Poland on 9–10 September 2025.

How it is drawn

  • A track is a path, a launch time and a speed; its position at the clock's time is walked along the path. The past is a solid line, the future dashed with times every 30 minutes, inside a cone that widens with time.
  • A region's state is the earliest time any group's path enters it in the next three hours; a region a group has left turns blue-grey for debris.
  • Blue crosses are interceptions; numbers on a group are how many are still flying.
  • Launch areas are broad hatched ellipses, not sites.

What a real version needs

  • For replays: a documented night's tracks from public reporting, with how sure each one is.
  • For Live: only an official alert feed per country, by its own regions. The model above is for exercises; it must never colour a region in Live.
  • Municipalities or safety regions instead of provinces for the Netherlands — the areas NL-Alert is sent to.
  • Sound: an exercise never plays an alarm.
