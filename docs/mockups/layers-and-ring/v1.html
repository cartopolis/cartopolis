Layers by where they are, and a ring of actions

Two proposals. The Layers panel today is one long list of about 40 checkboxes, three levels deep, ordered by when each layer was added. Here it becomes a sheet with six places to look: the ground, the city, places, things that move, what is under the ground, and what is in orbit. Each layer is a tile you tap. The text context menu becomes a ring of round buttons around the point you tapped, as drawn for people in chat-world v4. Nothing here is built yet; all of it is new.

Grouped by where, not by source

“Under the ground” and “in orbit” are places you can picture. “Overpass street detail” and “3DBAG” are not, so the source moves into a small line, and the NL badge says what only works in the Netherlands.

A tile is one tap

The whole tile is the switch, sized for a thumb. A tile with more inside (kinds of satellite, the eight underground layers) has ⋯: a long press or that corner opens its options in place.

Say when it can't be seen

A layer that is on but cannot show from here is dimmed with the reason: satellites from the street, sewers from 20 km up. That way, ticking a layer never seems to do nothing.

One ring, everywhere

The same ring opens on a point on the map, a building, an object, a person or a satellite. The main action is orange and sits at the top; ✕ is in the middle where your thumb already is. On the desktop it opens at the cursor.

7Ring: a satellite

  • Follow attaches the camera; Path shows the orbit for the next hour (on by default when one is picked); More opens the full card: over where, orbit period, NORAD number.
  • The details pill reads live, so altitude and speed change while you look.

8Following

  • The camera rides along 20 km away with the planet below. Drag turns around the satellite and pinch zooms, down to 200 m or out past the geostationary belt.
  • The chip at the top says what you follow; ✕ (or Esc) lets go and leaves you in Earth view exactly where you are.
  • Built so far as a general “follow this” that any layer can offer: satellites today, and later flights, ships, buses and friends.
