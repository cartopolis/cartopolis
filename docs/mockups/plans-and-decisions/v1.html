What is planned here, and who decided it

A proposal for a Plans layer: every official notice the municipality of Groningen publishes about a place — permit applications and decisions, demolition notices, events, traffic orders — as a dot on the spot it names, and the council decisions behind them one tap away. Every notice and date on this sheet is real: the 22 dots are the notices published 10 August – 29 October 2026 for the drawn part of the city centre, the photo is PDOK's current aerial, and the council decision is the Vismarkt maintenance credit of 18 February 2026. All of it is new.

The notice is the anchor

Groningen publishes ~450 notices a month in the Gemeenteblad, and 96 % carry a point, line or area. Those coordinates are what goes on the map; nothing is placed by guesswork.

Open first

An orange dot is a notice whose response period is still running: there is still time to object or comment. Grey ones are history. The count at the top says how many are still open here.

Council links say how they were made

Council documents have no coordinates. They are linked to a place by the street and square names in their text, and the link says so (matched by name), so a wrong match can be seen and reported.

Follow a place, not a feed

Follow your street or square and hear when something new is published within 100 m. That is the “is anything planned at all?” question, answered without having to look.

3A decision's story

4Anywhere: is anything planned?

  • With the layer off, the map's ring gets Plans here, and the pill under it counts what was published nearby — so the question can be asked of any spot, layer or not.
  • A followed place sends one notification per new notice within 100 m, here the real felling application at Zwanestraat 27 of 21 October.
  • The count needs no server work per tap: notices are a few thousand points a year, small enough to stream with the map.

The coordinates come with the notices, so the map side needs no geocoding. The council side does: a matcher from the names in a document to the places in BAG/OSM, with a review step, because a raadsvoorstel names many streets it is not about (the 2025 annual report names the Zuiderplantsoen). Open question: whether the end of the response period means the same for an application (comment) as for a decision (objection) — the notices use one field for both.
