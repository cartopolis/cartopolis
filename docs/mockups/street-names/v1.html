Street names — readable when you walk v1

Asked 2026-11-18: walking as the avatar, the street names cannot be read. Today a name is laid flat in the road (systems::street_labels), which reads well from above and not at all from 1.7 m: at eye height the road is seen almost edge-on, so the letters are squashed to a few pixels tall and turned sideways. Three ways to show the name at street level, all new. The name on the ground stays for every view from above.

0Now

  • The name lies in the road, read along the street. From eye height it is a white smear across the pavers.
  • Placed for the camera's heading, so it is sideways to you whenever you look down the street — which is how you walk.

AA street-name plate on the corner new

  • Where a real one hangs: on the corner building of every junction, ~3 m up, facing the street it names — the dark-blue Dutch plate. Both ends of each block.
  • Still in the world: it holds still, takes the perspective of the wall, and a building in front hides it — the rule street names follow today.
  • Drawn a little larger than life (about 1.5×) so a phone reads it from 20–30 m.
  • Shown only when the camera is low (the same height at which the road name fades).

BThe road name stands up new

  • As the camera comes down, the flat name tilts upright along the kerb, about 1 m tall, so it faces you at an angle instead of edge-on.
  • Same label, same placement, no new data — one rotation tied to camera height.
  • But at street level it is a large word standing in the street: you walk through it, and it covers what is behind it. Closer to the sticker-on-glass look the old screen labels were dropped for.

the street you are in

C"You are in" under the search bar new

  • One chip with the street the avatar stands in, and its neighbourhood. Changes as you cross into the next street.
  • About you, not about the street, so it may be an overlay — like the position line in a navigation app. Only when walking, at street level.
  • Tap it: the street's card (what is on it, its history, who is near).
  • Does not tell you the names of the side streets you pass — A does.

Proposed: A + C

Plates on the corners for the streets around you, as in a real town, and one chip for the street you are in, so the name is there even mid-block. The name in the road stays for the view from above and fades out once the camera is low enough that it can no longer be read. B is the cheapest to build, but at eye height it puts a word in the way of the street itself.
