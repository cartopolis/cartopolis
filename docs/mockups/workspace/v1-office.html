EThe office is a building new

  • The workspace's home is a real building. Ownership and floor plans already exist: one account owns the building, and its rooms are drawn in the building editor at /building/{pand}. A workspace borrows that building's rooms.
  • Plan is the floor plan seen from above, drawn by the web UI from the same design, so it is light, readable and works without starting the viewer. World is the building in 3D, with the floors you can walk through that the app already has.
  • An open room is a call you walk into. Studio has a running conversation; entering it is joining. Listen in joins muted, for half-attention.
  • A closed door means busy, as in a real office: the quiet room has a closed door, and entering means knocking. Your own status follows where you sit: in the quiet room you are on focus, at your desk you are available.
  • Desks: every member has one. Walking up to someone's desk asks them for a quick word, like stopping by. Joris's desk shows where he is now.
  • Guests come in through the front door: an invite link places them at the entrance and somebody lets them in. Nobody gets access to a channel by accident.

FA place is a search term new

  • Click a building, get its history in the workspace: every message with a point on it, every call held there, every note left on it, from all the channels you are in. Search for an address and you get the same.
  • Results are keyed to the building's BAG number, which every drawn building already carries. A point that was made "at a building" stays with that building even if the conversation drifted elsewhere.
  • Search happens on your own device. Private messages are encrypted, so the server cannot search them; each device keeps its own index of what it can read. The results come from three channels and one private group, each marked as such.
  • Notes new: a message left on a facade instead of in a stream. It shows up in the world for members of the channel it was posted in, nobody else.
  • Municipal notices (chat-world v5) show up in the same list, so the official status of the street sits beside what the team said about it.

Build order, smallest useful first

  • 1. A chat client in the web UI: workspaces, channels, threads, DMs, point cards, in the shared palette. The web UI needs the app's encryption to do this, built for the browser as its own small module, with keys in the same browser storage the viewer uses.
  • 2. World inside the page: the existing web viewer embedded in the main column, started on first use, showing the channel's points and area.
  • 3. Calls in the grid, through the browser's own WebRTC (webcall.js already does this for the viewer). UDP only, as today; a TURN fallback for networks that block UDP is a later option.
  • 4. Share my view: the cheapest of the world ideas (camera position over the call) and the one most obviously better than anything a normal work chat can do.
  • 5. Room calls with distance, then 6. the building as office, then 7. search by place and notes.

Open questions

  • What a workspace is. Proposed: a named set of encrypted group chats, plus a member list and a building. The server sees who is in which workspace and channel (it already sees chat membership), and nothing that is said.
  • A channel everybody in the workspace can join has to let a new member read back. With encryption that means a member's device hands over the history, or new members see only what comes after they joined.
  • Viewer download on first World. The viewer is the large part of the page, so it loads only when World is first opened and is cached after. To measure: how long the first open takes on an ordinary laptop.
  • Location in a work context. Real location stays per channel and until a set time, as in the app. A workspace setting to turn it off entirely is probably needed for employers.
  • On a phone the web UI should fall back to the app's own chat layout (chat-world v5) rather than squeeze this one down.
