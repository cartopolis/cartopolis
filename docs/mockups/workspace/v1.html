cartopolis workspace — a work chat with a world behind it

The web UI becomes the place a team talks: channels, threads, direct messages and calls, using the same encrypted chats and calls as the app. Every channel keeps the app's Chat | World switch, and World is the real viewer, running inside the page. The world is not a game mode bolted on: it is where — where the work is, where people are, where the team meets.

1Every channel has a place

A channel can be bound to an area (a project site, a street) or to a room in the team's own building. World takes you there, with the channel's points pinned on it.

2Presence says where, when you allow it

Beside online and away: ● in the world, at the Vismarkt or ● on site, until 17:00. Off by default, per channel, with an end time: the app's location-sharing rule.

3A call is a room

Every call has Grid | Room. In Room the call takes place in the channel's place: faces over avatars, quieter with distance, and you walk to a corner to break out.

4Show the place instead of sharing a screen

Share my view lends your camera to the call: everyone's viewer follows yours, live and sharp. Anyone can look around on their own and snap back. Points dropped on the way are posted to the chat.

5Things said about a place stay there

Search by place ("everything about Noorderhaven 3"), and notes left on a building that anyone in the channel can see when they get there.

6The office is a building

The team's home is a real building in the world, its floor plan drawn in the building editor. Its rooms are the team's open rooms; the sidebar shows who is in which.

Branding: halfway between the web UI and the app

The web UI today is slate with the full brand orange; the app (systems::theme) is charcoal with a softer ember. Each value proposed here is the midpoint of the two, so both move once to meet in the middle. Font (Plus Jakarta Sans), wordmark (cream carto + brand polis), the brand orange kept for the logo, badges and live dot, and the real-location blue all stay as they are.

AChat: a work chat first

  • The layout people already know: workspaces in the rail, channels and DMs in the sidebar, threads in a right pane. Nothing about the world needs learning to use it as a chat.
  • Same chats as the app. A workspace channel is an encrypted group chat, so it shows up in the app too and the padlock means the same thing. The server stores what it cannot read.
  • Points are the app's point messages: a place plus the sender's camera. Open in World puts you where they stood.
  • Presence has a where new: orange is someone's avatar in the world, blue is someone's real position, which they shared with this workspace until a time they chose. Most people, most of the time, are neither.
  • Rooms in the building new: open rooms anyone in the workspace can drop into, with who is in each (sheet 3).
  • The thread holds a sketch block: Sanne placed a block where the shed could go, and the discussion is about it, not about a drawing of it.

BWorld: the same channel, in the viewer

  • The main column becomes the viewer. Sidebar and chat stay where they were, so switching to World swaps the middle and moves nothing you were using.
  • It is the app's viewer, the existing web build in the page rather than a link to /viewer/. It starts the first time you open World and then keeps running, so switching back and forth is instant. While it starts, the point's picture stands in for it.
  • What's on it is this channel only: its area new, its numbered points, and the members who chose to show themselves here. A new message pops up over its sender and lands in the chat pane at the same time.
  • Point at… in the composer: click anything in the world (a building, a window, a spot on the street) and it becomes the next message, seen from where you stand.
  • Clicking a point in the sidebar flies the camera to the sender's view. Chat brings you back to the same message.
