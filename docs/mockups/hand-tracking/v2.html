Your hand, through the phone

"High precision" is really about your hand: the phone you hold becomes the phone in your avatar's hand, and moving it moves the avatar's arm. So it leaves the locate button, which is about where you are, and becomes a hand button of its own beside the face button. The camera's selfie is the first place that switches it on by itself; anything that senses hands, such as the theremin, offers it when you walk up.

Proposal v2, 2026-10-28: the Mixed selfie added, the hand placed by the phone's turn and your face in every selfie. Grey controls are the app as it is today; anything marked new does not exist yet. The avatar pictures are Cartopolis captures of the arm that landed on 2026-10-27 (systems::held_phone), in its fixed test poses.

1Out of the locate menu, into a hand button

Today the mode is a checkbox in the locate button's long-press menu, worded as positioning to centimetres, which it does not do yet. What it does do is follow the phone in your hand, to about a centimetre relative to where it started.

High precision (camera)

Pins your position down to centimetres with the back camera and the buildings around you. The positioning itself is not built yet; Developer tools ▸ Precision shows what the sensors report.

Newa hand button, and a mirror new

  • The hand button sits above the face button in the touch column, both things your avatar does with your body. A tap turns it on or off; it is lit while your hand is followed.
  • A round mirror shows your avatar from the front while it is on, since the camera behind you cannot see your hand. It uses the small avatar camera that "Show your avatar instead" in calls already has. Tap it to hide it.
  • Locate goes back to being about where you are: its menu loses the checkbox. A receiver's accuracy (±2 cm RTK) stays under its arrow.

4In the camera: the selfie holds the phone new

In a selfie the phone always looks back at your face from arm's length. So how the phone is turned already says where it is, on a sphere round your head, and the front camera, which sees your face, says how far. No back camera is needed: the front camera takes the photograph, reads your expression, and measures the distance, all from one picture stream.

Mixed selfieyou on one half, your avatar on the other

  • Real: the front camera's photograph of you. Cartopolis: the world drawn from exactly where that camera is — your position, the phone's turn, your face's distance — looking back at your avatar.
  • Your avatar holds the phone toward the lens, its arm reaching out of the bottom corner as yours does, and turns its body to face the phone. Move the phone round and both halves swing round together.
  • Its face is yours: the same front camera reads your expression (built, face_tracking), so a laugh on the left is a laugh on the right.
  • One picture comes out with both halves, and its pose (EXIF) is the phone's, as every Mixed picture's is.
  • The Cartopolis half here is a stand-in: the avatar in its selfie test pose seen from 1.5 m, the nearest the capture camera goes. Built, the view is the phone's own, so the phone is out of shot and the avatar looks into the lens.

Howthe phone's turn and your face place the hand

  • The world camera and the avatar's hand are put at the same point: the phone.
  • The hand follows the phone without the back camera, so this works on any phone with a front camera and motion sensors; it is the button's selfie state.

5Where else the hand is useful

The button is not the camera's: anything can read the hand. In order of what exists to build on.

Playing things

A theremin, a drum, a bell: prims that call sense_hands work today. Built (the prompt is new).

Waving in a call

"Show your avatar instead" films the avatar; with the hand on, it waves and points with you.

Showing others

Point at a building and people nearby see your arm. Needs the arm on the wire, rate-limited like a movement intent. Step 4.

Placing things by hand

Put a prim where the phone is, instead of tapping the ground: the phone as the hand that sets it down.

6What goes where

Open: whether the hand stays on by itself next time the app starts (proposed: no, like the camera); whether the mirror is on by default (proposed: yes, for the first minute); the body behind the hand (step 2) is what makes the absolute place right, and is not drawn here.
