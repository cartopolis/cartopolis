The zoom ladder v4: the scale switch folded into one disc

v4, after review the same day: the scale switch is one disc on desktop too, as on the phone — it wears the current scale's icon and opens the four on a tap. Everything else is v3.
v3: asked 2026-11-07: the zoom rail is impractical — one slider from the street to the edge of the universe gives each rung a few points of track, and a slip of the thumb throws you out of the Earth. The rail should span the scale you are in — the globe, the solar system, the Milky Way, the universe — and moving between scales should stay logical, but not be part of the one slider. The pictures are today's client (captured on mac-2); the controls on them are drawn. Orange marks what is new.

A scale is a place, not a zoom level

Four scales, each with its own subject in the middle: your place, the Sun, the galaxy, the cosmic web. The switch says where you are; the rail says how close.

The rail stops at the scale's edge

A drag, a pinch, the wheel or a held − can never leave the scale by accident. The rail's ends are real ends.

The edge offers the next scale

At the top, − turns into the next scale's button. Tap it, or keep zooming out for a moment, and you go on — seamlessly, as today.

A tap on a scale flies there

Through every scale in between, as a rung tap does today. The globe returns you to the place and height you left.

3Leaving a scale at its edge new

Zooming out of the globe with a pinch or the wheel. The rail runs out at World; there the zoom stops with a small give, and the − at the top of the rail becomes the next scale's button. Going on is a choice, but not a detour: tap it, or keep zooming out for half a second. Zooming in at the foot of a scale works the same way downwards, with the + becoming the scale below.

1 · Zooming outContinent, near the top of the Globe's rail. Nothing new to see.
2 · The end of the railAt World the zoom stops, with a small spring back. − is now Solar system, in orange, its name beside it.
3 · Keep pushingPinching or scrolling on fills the ring round it; letting go before it is full leaves you on the globe. A tap goes at once.
4 · In the solar systemThe switch moves on, the rail is the solar system's, the thumb at its foot — the view went on from where the globe stopped, as it does today.

Why the edge does not simply let you through

  • That is today's slider with a line drawn on it: a fling that overshoots still lands among the planets, and coming back means finding the way down again.
  • A hard wall with no way on would make the scales separate apps. The − that becomes the next scale keeps the climb in the hand that is already zooming.
  • Half a second of pushing on is long enough that a wheel's overrun or a pinch's last bit of travel does not cross, short enough that a deliberate climb is not a chore.

The rest of the movement

  • Over the edge the view goes on from where it was: out of the globe at its ceiling into Earth & Moon, down from Earth & Moon onto the globe where you left it.
  • A tap on a scale flies through the ones between, at today's flight rates (street to universe in about five seconds); the switch lights each one as it passes.
  • A tap on the current scale goes to its overview: the whole Earth, the eight planets, the spiral, the web.
  • Keys: − / + zoom within the scale and stop at its ends, PgUp / PgDn change scale.
