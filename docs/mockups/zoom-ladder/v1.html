One zoom from the street to the Milky Way new

Asked for 2026-11-01: where are the solar system and the Milky Way? Moving from one layer to the next should be seamless; turning the globe is laborious; the real sun and its night side are beautiful but get in the way of finding a place. Today the camera stops at about 38,600 km, the globe stops dead when you let go, a jump is an instant cut, and above the globe there is nothing but the sky dome. This sheet proposes one continuous zoom with a rail that names where you are, a globe you can grab and throw, light that serves the reader, and three new rungs above the Earth.

One zoom, all the way up

Wheel, pinch or the rail take you from your door to the galaxy without a mode switch. Each rung fades into the next on height, as the globe already does.

The rail says where you are

A thin ladder on the right edge names the rung you are on. Tap a rung to fly there; drag along it to zoom. It replaces the + / − pair.

Grab the globe

The place under your finger stays under your finger. Let go while moving and it keeps turning. Double-tap to go closer to a place.

Earth is home

Zooming in anywhere above the Moon heads back to the Earth, and lands where you left. You cannot get lost among the stars.

2The way up: three new rungs above the Earth

Past the globe the Earth shrinks to a disc, then to a node. Above the World rung distances are no longer drawn to scale (a planet would be a sub-pixel dot); each rung is a board of real bodies at real angles, with log-scaled distances, as on the scenarios v3 sheet. The camera stays pointed at home until you pan away.

Seamless, not a series of screens

  • One number drives it all: the camera's height, from 15 m to 1021 m. Each rung crossfades into the next over its band, never switches.
  • The node a board is centred on is the thing you came from: the Earth in the solar system, Sol among the stars, Sol's place in the galaxy. Zoom out from anywhere and home stays in view.
  • A rung's name appears for a moment at the top as you cross into it ("Solar system · light from the Sun takes 8 min 20 s").

The way down always lands

  • Above the Moon, zooming in pulls towards the Earth whatever is under the pointer; a planet or a star is a card, not a door. Visiting Mars is a later decision.
  • Coming back down, the globe turns to the place you left and the camera lands at the height you left from — not at whatever was under the pointer 1020 m up.
  • Real data only: JPL's mean orbital elements for the planets, the Moon from the sky code's own formulae, HYG for the stars. Fiction (scenarios v3's sandbox) stays out of this.

3Turning the globe

What each hand does, above about 50 km where the globe takes over from the ground. Below it nothing changes.

Grab

Dragging turns the globe so the point you pressed stays under the pointer, at every height. Drag off the edge of the disc and the globe turns round its own axis instead.

Today: a turn rate set from the height, so the place you grabbed drifts from the finger.

Throw

Let go while moving and the globe keeps turning, slowing to a stop in about a second. One flick crosses an ocean; a touch catches it.

Today: stops dead on release; crossing to Japan takes four or five drags.

Double-tap

Double-tap (double-click) flies four times closer, centred on that place. Two-finger tap goes four times out. A tapped name label flies to the place itself.

Today: nothing; only the wheel, pinch or + / −.

Turn, tilt, keys

Right-drag turns and tilts (two-finger twist on a phone). Arrow keys turn the globe, + and − zoom, N or the compass puts north up.

Today: middle-drag or Ctrl-drag; the right button does nothing; no keys.

5Flights instead of cuts, and the rail on a phone

Every jump — a rung on the rail, a search result, locate, Back — becomes one flight: up the ladder until both ends fit on screen, across, and down again (the zoom-and-pan path of van Wijk & Nuij, 2003). It takes 1 to 4 seconds depending on how far it goes, and any touch stops it where it is. The rung names pass by at the top, so you see how far you went.

Rules

  • Height is the one control. The rail, the wheel, pinch and flights all move the same number; no rung is a mode you have to leave.
  • Every jump is a flight you can interrupt; nothing teleports the view in front of you (a Teleport still happens underneath, out of sight, when the flight lands).
  • The globe obeys the hand: the grabbed point stays under it; a throw keeps turning; a touch stops.
  • Light from above is for reading by default; the real sun is one tap away and is the only light above the World rung.
  • Above the World rung only real bodies at real angles; distances are log-scaled and say so.

Build order, each step useful alone

  • 1. The globe in the hand: grab, throw, double-tap, right-drag, keys; the even light and its switch.
  • 2. Flights: one flight for the rail, search, locate and Back.
  • 3. The rail, folded on phones, over today's rungs.
  • 4. Earth & Moon: the ceiling raised past the Moon, the Moon a real body.
  • 5. Solar system, Stars, Milky Way as boards above it, with planet and star cards.
